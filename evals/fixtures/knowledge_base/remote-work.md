# Remote Work Policy

This policy describes how remote and hybrid work is expected to function
across the company. It applies to every employee whose role does not
require physical presence at a specific location, such as running lab
equipment or staffing a retail counter.

The policy is reviewed by People Operations once a year, and more often if
a change in law or a company-wide shift in working model requires it.
Managers may set team-specific norms within this policy, such as a
preferred meeting-free day, but may not override the limits this policy
sets.

## Eligibility and schedule

Employees may work remotely up to three days per week with their manager's
approval; some roles are fully remote by design and are not bound by this
three-day figure, as specified in their offer letter. Days worked in the
office should be agreed with the immediate team so that in-person
collaboration time is actually shared rather than scattered across
different days for different people.

Employees who wish to work from a different city or country than their
home location for an extended period, such as a month or more, must submit
a request through the HR portal so that payroll, tax, and benefits
implications can be reviewed before the move; approval is not automatic and
depends on whether the company has the ability to legally employ someone in
that location.

## Equipment and expenses

Employees working remotely are provided with a laptop and, on request, a
monitor and an ergonomic chair for their home setup. Equipment requests go
through the IT request form described in the Employee Handbook. A one-time
stipend is available for a home internet upgrade if the employee's existing
connection does not meet the minimum bandwidth the IT team specifies for
video calls and remote development work.

## Access to systems

Remote work does not change any of the access rules described in the
Security Policy. VPN access is required to reach internal-only systems from
outside the office network, and engineers who need production database
access while working remotely still request it through the standard access
request form and still need production database access sign-off from the
data owner and an engineering manager, exactly as they would from the
office.

## Availability and communication

Remote employees are expected to be reachable during their team's agreed
core hours and to keep their calendar accurate so meetings can be scheduled
without back-and-forth. Video should be on by default for team meetings
unless there is a specific reason not to, since it meaningfully changes how
a distributed conversation feels compared to audio only.

Asynchronous communication is the default for anything that does not need
a live conversation. Writing a clear, self-contained message that someone
in a different time zone can act on without needing to ask a clarifying
question first is treated as a core skill, not an optional nicety.

## Security expectations while remote

Employees working from a coffee shop, co-working space, or any network they
do not control must use the company VPN and must not leave a company
laptop unattended and unlocked, following the same device handling rules
described in the Security Policy. Company data should never be printed at
a public print shop or left visible on a screen in a space where it could
be read by someone else.

## In-office expectations

Teams that choose a shared in-office day are expected to actually be
present on that day rather than treating it as optional; managers are
responsible for holding their team to whatever schedule the team agreed on.
Desks are not permanently assigned in most offices, and employees should
check the desk booking tool before a planned in-office day to confirm space
is available.

## Performance and remote work

Remote and hybrid employees are evaluated using the exact same performance
criteria described in the Employee Handbook; working location is not a
factor in a performance rating. Managers of distributed teams are expected
to create deliberate opportunities for visibility into remote employees'
work, such as regular demos or written updates, rather than defaulting to
whoever is physically in the office being more visible by accident.

## International remote work and taxes

An employee working from a country other than the one they were hired in,
even temporarily, can create tax and legal obligations for the company in
that country. This is why the extended-relocation request described above
exists even for moves the employee considers short-term; a request that
seems like a formality to the employee may have real payroll and
compliance implications that People Operations and Legal need to assess in
advance, not after the move has already happened.

Short trips of a few weeks for personal travel while continuing to work do
not require the extended-relocation approval, but employees are still
asked to inform their manager and People Operations of extended travel so
the company has an accurate picture of where its workforce is located at
any given time, which matters for both tax purposes and for emergency
communication.

## Meeting norms across time zones

Teams spanning more than a small number of time zones are expected to
rotate meeting times so that the same group of people is not always the
one waking up early or staying up late for a recurring meeting. Meetings
that do not require live discussion should default to an asynchronous
written update instead, consistent with the asynchronous-by-default
principle described above.

## Home office stipend details

The home office equipment described above is a one-time setup rather than
a recurring benefit; employees who need a replacement due to normal wear
should route the request through the standard IT request form rather than
treating the original stipend as an annual allowance. Employees who leave
the company are not required to return furniture purchased through the
stipend, unlike company-issued electronics, which must be returned as
described in the Employee Handbook's offboarding section.

## Coworking space reimbursement

Employees without a suitable home working environment may request
reimbursement for a coworking space membership, subject to manager
approval and the standard expense thresholds described in the Expense
Policy. This is treated as a business expense rather than part of the
one-time equipment stipend, since it is a recurring cost rather than a
one-time purchase.
