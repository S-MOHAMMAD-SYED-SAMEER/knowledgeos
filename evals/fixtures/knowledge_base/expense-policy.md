# Expense Policy

This policy describes what business expenses the company reimburses, how
to submit them, and what approval each type of expense requires. It is
owned by Finance and applies to every employee, regardless of department or
level, though certain categories such as travel have role-specific
thresholds noted below.

Expenses that are not covered by this policy can still be reimbursed with
prior written approval from a manager and Finance, but that approval must
be obtained before the expense is incurred, not after. Submitting a receipt
for an unapproved category after the fact is not a guarantee of
reimbursement.

## Submitting an expense

Expenses are submitted through the expense system with a receipt attached,
a business justification, and the relevant cost center. Expenses under one
hundred dollars require only manager approval. Expenses between one hundred
and one thousand dollars require manager approval and are spot-checked by
Finance. Expenses above one thousand dollars require pre-approval from a
manager before the expense is incurred, not only after-the-fact submission.

Expense reports should be submitted within thirty days of the expense being
incurred. Reports submitted more than ninety days after the expense may be
denied at Finance's discretion, since older receipts are harder to verify
and older reports create tax and accounting complications for the current
period.

## Travel

Airfare should be booked in economy class for flights under six hours and
may be booked in premium economy for longer flights, subject to manager
approval. Hotel bookings should stay within the per-city rate guide
published by Finance; a booking above the guide rate requires a brief
justification, such as the guide-rate hotels being fully booked for the
travel dates.

Ground transportation, including rideshare and public transit, is
reimbursable for business travel. Rental cars require manager approval and
should be booked through the corporate travel portal so that the company's
insurance coverage applies automatically.

## Meals

Meals while traveling for business are reimbursable up to a daily per diem
published by Finance, without requiring itemized receipts below a small
threshold; anything above that threshold requires an itemized receipt, not
just a card statement line. Client meals are reimbursable at actual cost
with a business justification naming who attended and the purpose of the
meeting, and do not count against the traveling employee's own per diem.

Alcohol is reimbursable only as part of a client meal and only within
reason; a bottle of wine at dinner with a client is fine, a bar tab after
the client has left is not.

## Software and subscriptions

Individual software subscriptions needed for a role should be requested
through IT rather than expensed directly, so the company maintains one
record of what is licensed to whom, which also matters for the offboarding
process described in the Employee Handbook. A subscription expensed
directly without going through IT may be denied even if the tool itself
would have been approved, because the point of the rule is the record, not
the specific approval path.

## What is not reimbursable

Personal entertainment, fines and parking tickets, and expenses for a
spouse or family member accompanying an employee on a business trip are not
reimbursable. Upgrades purely for personal comfort, such as paying
out-of-pocket to upgrade a flight and then expensing a portion of it, are
also not reimbursable; the company will book or reimburse the approved
class of travel and nothing above it.

## Corporate cards

Employees who travel frequently or manage a team budget may be issued a
corporate card. Corporate card transactions still require the same
documentation as a reimbursement claim; a corporate card does not remove
the requirement to attach a receipt and a business justification, it only
changes who fronts the money.

## Mileage and personal vehicle use

Employees who use a personal vehicle for business purposes, such as
driving to a client site, are reimbursed at the standard mileage rate
published by Finance each year, calculated from the expense system's
built-in mileage calculator rather than a manually estimated distance.
Parking and tolls incurred during business use of a personal vehicle are
reimbursable separately from the mileage rate itself.

## Team and department expenses

Team lunches, off-sites, and department celebrations are budgeted
separately from individual expense limits and are typically pre-approved
as part of a team's quarterly budget rather than submitted one event at a
time. A manager organizing a team event should confirm the spend is within
the team's remaining budget before committing to a venue, since Finance
does not pre-approve individual team events beyond the quarterly budget
allocation.

## Relocation expenses

Employees relocating for a new role within the company, as opposed to the
extended remote-work travel described in the Remote Work Policy, may be
eligible for relocation assistance covering moving costs, temporary
housing, and related expenses, subject to a separate relocation agreement
negotiated with People Operations rather than the standard expense
thresholds in this policy. Relocation assistance is typically subject to a
repayment clause if the employee leaves the company within a defined
period after the move.

## Currency and international expenses

Expenses incurred in a currency other than the employee's home currency
are converted using the exchange rate on the date of the transaction, as
recorded by the expense system, not the date the report is submitted.
Employees traveling internationally for business are encouraged to use a
corporate card where possible to avoid unfavorable personal card foreign
transaction fees, which are not separately reimbursed above the actual
converted expense amount.

## Audits and policy violations

Finance periodically audits submitted expenses for compliance with this
policy, in addition to the standard spot-checking described above.
Repeated policy violations, such as consistently submitting client meals
without the required attendee list, may result in a manager being asked to
review and pre-approve that employee's expenses individually for a period,
separate from any broader disciplinary process under the Employee
Handbook's code of conduct.
