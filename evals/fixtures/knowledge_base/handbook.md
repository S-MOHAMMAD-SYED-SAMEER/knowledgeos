# Employee Handbook

This handbook describes the policies and expectations that apply to every
employee of the company, regardless of department, level, or location. It is
reviewed annually by People Operations and updated whenever a policy
materially changes. Where this handbook conflicts with a more specific
policy document, such as the Security Policy or the Remote Work Policy, the
more specific document governs.

New hires receive a copy of this handbook during onboarding and are asked to
acknowledge that they have read it within their first week. Managers are
expected to walk new team members through the sections most relevant to
their role during the first one-on-one meeting.

## Working hours and time off

Standard working hours are ten to six, Monday through Friday, in the
employee's local time zone. Teams that need broader overlap with
international colleagues may agree on a different core schedule with their
manager's approval, but any such arrangement must be documented in the HR
system so payroll and benefits calculations stay accurate.

Employees accrue fifteen days of paid time off per year during their first
three years of employment, rising to twenty days after three years and
twenty-five days after seven years. Time off requests should be submitted at
least two weeks in advance through the HR portal, except in the case of
illness or family emergency, where same-day notice to a manager is
sufficient and the request can be filed retroactively.

Unused time off may be carried over into the following year up to a maximum
of five days; anything beyond that is paid out at year end according to the
jurisdiction's rules. Sick leave is tracked separately from vacation time
and is not subject to the carryover cap.

## Compensation and performance

Compensation is reviewed twice a year, in April and October, alongside a
formal performance conversation with the employee's manager. Performance is
assessed against goals set at the start of each half and against the
company's stated leveling criteria, which are published internally and
apply uniformly across departments.

Bonuses, where applicable, are calculated from a combination of individual
performance rating and overall company performance for the period. The
exact formula is reviewed by Finance and People Operations each cycle and
communicated to managers before compensation conversations begin, so that
no manager is negotiating from an outdated formula.

## Code of conduct

Every employee is expected to treat colleagues, customers, and partners
with respect. Harassment, discrimination, and retaliation of any kind are
prohibited and will result in disciplinary action up to and including
termination. Employees who witness or experience a violation of this policy
should report it to their manager, to People Operations, or through the
anonymous ethics hotline described in the Security Policy.

Conflicts of interest — financial, familial, or otherwise — must be
disclosed to a manager or to People Operations as soon as they become
apparent. Employees may not use company resources, including equipment,
company time, or confidential information, for personal or outside business
purposes without prior written approval.

## Equipment and expenses

Every employee is issued a laptop and any peripherals reasonably required
for their role. Equipment remains company property and must be returned
upon departure. Requests for non-standard equipment go through the IT
request form and require manager approval.

Business expenses, including travel, client meals, and approved
subscriptions, are reimbursed through the expense system described in the
Expense Policy. Employees should not use personal funds for large purchases
without pre-approval, since reimbursement above the standard thresholds
requires sign-off before the expense is incurred, not after.

## Departure and offboarding

Employees who resign are asked to provide at least two weeks' notice in
writing to their manager and to People Operations. During the notice
period, access to sensitive systems may be reduced ahead of the final day
as part of standard offboarding procedure, which is not a reflection of
trust but a routine control described in the Security Policy.

On the final day, all company equipment must be returned, and access to
company systems — email, source control, the document knowledge base, and
any department-specific tools — is revoked at end of business. Final
paycheck and any accrued, unused vacation payout are processed according to
the applicable jurisdiction's timeline, typically within the next regular
payroll cycle.

## Learning and development

Every employee has an annual learning budget that can be spent on
conferences, courses, books, or certifications relevant to their role. The
budget does not roll over between years and must be requested through the
same expense system used for other reimbursements, with a manager's
approval confirming the relevance to the employee's role.

Internal mobility — moving to a different team or role within the company —
is encouraged and does not require a manager's permission to explore,
though a manager should be told once a move is being seriously considered
rather than finding out after an offer is already made. People Operations
maintains a lightweight internal transfer process separate from external
hiring so an internal move does not have to go through the full external
interview loop.

## Benefits

Health, dental, and vision coverage begins on the first day of employment,
with the company covering a majority of the premium for the employee and a
partial contribution toward dependents, varying by plan and jurisdiction.
Enrollment details are sent during onboarding, as described in the
Onboarding Guide, and can be changed during the annual open enrollment
period or after a qualifying life event such as marriage or the birth of a
child.

Retirement plan contributions are matched by the company up to a
percentage of salary described in the benefits summary, with matching
contributions vesting immediately rather than over a multi-year schedule.
Parental leave provides a fixed number of paid weeks regardless of which
parent is the primary caregiver, and can be taken in a single continuous
block or split within the first year after the child's arrival, subject to
manager coordination on timing.

## Workplace safety

Employees are expected to report any workplace safety concern, whether in
an office or in the course of business travel, to People Operations
immediately rather than waiting for a scheduled check-in. This includes
both physical safety concerns and situations that feel unsafe for other
reasons, such as an uncomfortable interaction with a colleague or a vendor.

Offices maintain standard safety equipment and emergency procedures posted
in common areas, and new hires are shown the relevant exits and assembly
points as part of their first-day orientation described in the Onboarding
Guide.
